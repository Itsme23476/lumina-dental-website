Quick check

How urgent is it?

Answer a few yes/no questions for a recommendation. This tool is a guide, not a diagnosis. If in doubt, call us.

Call us right away for…

  • A knocked-out or loosened adult tooth
  • Severe toothache that keeps you awake
  • Swelling of the gums, face or jaw
  • A painful abscess or pimple on the gum
  • Bleeding that won't stop after 20 minutes of pressure

Usually can wait 24–48 hours

  • A small chip with no pain
  • A lost filling or crown without severe pain
  • Mild sensitivity to hot or cold
  • A loose bracket or poking braces wire
  • Food stuck between teeth
First aid

What to do right now

  1. Pick the tooth up by the crown (the white part). Don't touch the root.
  2. If it's dirty, rinse it briefly with milk or water. Don't scrub it or wrap it in tissue.
  3. If you can, gently push it back into the socket and bite on a clean cloth.
  4. If not, keep it in a cup of milk or inside your cheek (adults only).
  5. Call us immediately. The best chance of saving the tooth is within 30–60 minutes.

Baby teeth: don't try to put a knocked-out baby tooth back in. Call us so we can check for damage.

  1. Rinse your mouth with warm water.
  2. Gently floss to remove any trapped food.
  3. Hold a cold compress against your cheek for 15 minutes at a time.
  4. Take an over-the-counter pain reliever as directed on the label.
  5. Call us for a same-day appointment.

Don't place aspirin or painkillers directly on the gum. It can burn the tissue.

  1. Save any broken pieces in milk or a clean container.
  2. Rinse your mouth with warm water.
  3. Use a cold compress to reduce swelling.
  4. Cover sharp edges with sugar-free gum or dental wax.
  5. Call us. Most chips can be repaired in one visit.

Avoid chewing on that side until you've been seen.

  1. Keep the crown somewhere safe and bring it with you.
  2. Clean the area gently and avoid very hot, cold or sweet foods.
  3. Pharmacy dental cement can temporarily cover the area.
  4. Book a visit within a day or two.

Never use household glue to reattach a crown.

  1. Rinse with mild salt water (½ teaspoon of salt in a glass of warm water).
  2. Apply a cold compress to the outside of your face.
  3. Don't try to pop or drain an abscess yourself.
  4. Call us the same day. Infections need prompt treatment.

Go to the ER if swelling spreads to your eye or neck, or you have a fever and trouble swallowing or breathing.

  1. Push a poking wire gently back with the eraser end of a pencil.
  2. Cover sharp ends with orthodontic wax.
  3. Save any loose brackets and bring them to your visit.
  4. Call us to schedule a repair.

Never cut a wire yourself unless instructed by our team.
